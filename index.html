Frequently asked questions

What does the AI actually do?

It outpaints: the AI studies your photo's content, lighting and style, then generates new pixels around the edges to fill the larger canvas. Your original photo is never altered or cropped.

What does the Sharpen tool do?

It reconstructs pixelated, blurry or soft photos with AI. 1× cleans up at the original size, 2× / 4× enlarge while rebuilding detail, and Face enhance gives portraits extra care. One honest note: detail is re-imagined, not recovered — the AI invents plausible sharpness, it can't bring back what was never captured. 1 credit per photo, from the same wallet as Extend.

What does the Unblur tool do?

It reverses blur — camera shake, missed focus, motion smear — using a dedicated deblurring AI (not just edge sharpening). Same size in, same size out, 1 credit per photo. Honest note: it recovers a lot, but a photo that's extremely blurred has no detail left to recover — the before/after slider on every result shows you exactly what changed.

AI photo enhancement guides

Short, practical explainers on getting the most out of each tool — what the AI actually does, when to use which one, and how creators fit them into real work.

What is AI image extending (outpainting)?

AI image extending — also called outpainting or uncropping — is the opposite of cropping. Instead of cutting pixels away, the AI studies your photo's content, lighting, colors and style, then generates brand-new pixels beyond the original edges to fill a larger canvas.

A classic example: you have a square product photo but need a 16:9 YouTube thumbnail. A basic editor would stretch it (distortion) or leave empty bars. An AI image extender paints the missing sides — continuing the background, the sunset, the room — so the result looks like the photo was always that wide.

JSM Enhance's Extend tool works exactly this way. Upload any JPG, PNG or WebP, pick a target size — a preset like YouTube 1920×1080 or anything custom up to 4096px — and optionally describe the scene ("sunny beach with palm trees") to guide the AI. Your original photo is never altered, cropped or stretched: the AI only invents the new areas around it.

Two tips for the best results: keep expansions modest (4:3 → 16:9 looks more natural than square → tall 9:16, which asks the AI to invent a lot of canvas), and use the description box — a few words about the scene dramatically improve coherence.

Sharpen vs. Unblur: which AI tool fixes your photo?

Blurry photos come in two flavors, and they need different AI.

Choose Sharpen when your photo is pixelated, low-resolution or just soft — an old thumbnail, a heavily compressed chat image, a screenshot you want to print. Sharpen reconstructs detail with AI: 1× cleans up at the original size, 2× and 4× upscale while rebuilding sharpness (a true AI image upscaler), and Face enhance gives portraits special treatment. One honest note: the detail is re-imagined, not recovered — the AI invents plausible sharpness; it can't bring back what was never captured.

Choose Unblur when your photo suffers from motion blur or missed focus — camera shake, a moving subject, a slightly out-of-focus shot. Unblur uses a dedicated deblurring AI that mathematically reverses blur (not just edge contrast like a basic "sharpen" filter). Same size in, same size out.

Not sure? Try Sharpen first for small or soft images, Unblur first for shaky ones. Every result comes with a draggable before/after slider, so you can judge exactly what changed before you download.
